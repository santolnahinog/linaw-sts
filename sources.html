How we worked, and what to keep in mind

Honesty about limits is part of the design. These are the rules we followed.

Sources firstWhere a government body or international organisation published a figure, we used it. Otherwise we cross-checked at least two news outlets.
Sample data is randomThe Tracker's projects, firms and places are generated by a program. They are not real, and the Shuffle button changes them completely.
The risk points are our own designThe five warning signs come from documented procurement red flags, but the points (25, 15, 25, 25 and 10) are choices we made for a prototype. They are not an official or validated method.
Allegations stay allegationsThe cases we cite are alleged or under investigation. We do not name individuals, and anyone accused is presumed innocent.
No personal dataLinaw has no accounts, no tracking and no server. The forms are processed in your own browser and nothing is stored or sent.
Last checked October 2026News and agency details change. Items marked Check below need confirming against an official source before you rely on them.

Where Linaw could go next

These are ideas, not promises. Each one comes with its honest challenge, because a realistic plan names what could go wrong.

Next

Connect real project data

Replace the sample records with open data from the DPWH Transparency Portal, with clear attribution, so the Tracker shows real projects.

Challenge: depends on the data being published for reuse, and on keeping it current.

Next

Filipino and regional languages

Translate the interface, quiz and report guide into Filipino, then into major regional languages.

Challenge: procurement terms need careful, consistent translation, ideally reviewed by native speakers.

Next

Light, offline-friendly version

Make the site installable and usable with weak or no signal, and offer a text-message summary for basic phones.

Challenge: cached data can go stale, so the site must show when it was last updated.

Later

An auditor-validated risk model

Work with auditors and universities to test whether the warning signs and their points really predict problems, and to measure false alarms.

Challenge: needs real, labelled cases, and expert time we do not have yet.

Later

Barangay help desks and school partners

Train volunteers, student groups and barangay staff to help neighbours who are offline or unsure how to read a project record.

Challenge: needs trusted partners and a plan to keep volunteers safe.

Vision

Satellite and photo evidence

Show satellite imagery next to a project record so people can compare what was reported with what the ground shows.

Challenge: depends on partnerships such as PhilSA, and needs a privacy and accuracy review.

Alongside these, every release would need an accessibility audit with screen-reader users and a legal review under the Data Privacy Act.
