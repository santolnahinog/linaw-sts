Risk reading: High, verify on the ground

Most public projects can't be checked by the people who pay for them.

Every road, flood wall and classroom is paid for with public money. But who won the contract, what it cost, and whether it was built are scattered, vague, or hard to read. Where nobody can check, ghost projects, padded costs and favoured contractors can last for years.

Ghost project. A project reported as done, and paid for, that was never built or can't be found.

Follow one peso through five checkpoints.

Corruption rarely happens in one step. It slips in at points where the public can't see what is going on. Each checkpoint below is also a place where a citizen, with the right information, can ask a better question.

  1. 1

    Budget

    Where it goes wrong

    Project descriptions are vague, so nobody can tell what will really be built. Reports flagged over ₱350 billion in projects with unclear specifications.

    How you check

    Is there a place, a size and a purpose? A specific project is easier to verify.

  2. 2

    Bidding

    Where it goes wrong

    Few firms compete, or the same few keep winning. Nearly ₱100 billion went to 15 contractors.

    How you check

    How many bid, and who keeps winning in your area?

  3. 3

    Construction

    Where it goes wrong

    The work is poor, partly done, or never started. Officials confirmed 421 ghost projects.

    How you check

    Go and look. A dated photo from a safe distance is strong evidence.

  4. 4

    Payment

    Where it goes wrong

    Money is released for work reported as complete, whether or not it is.

    How you check

    Compare the reported status and cost with what you can see on site.

  5. 5

    Audit

    Where it goes wrong

    Problems surface late, often only after floods or whistleblowers force a look.

    How you check

    Send a clear report with evidence to the official channels that can investigate.

    Technology doesn't fix corruption by itself.

    A portal, a map, or an app only helps if people can use it and if the data behind it is honest. That is the Science, Technology and Society question at the heart of this project.

    Technology reflects who built it

    A transparency portal shows only the data its builders chose to publish. Design decisions are political decisions.

    Social construction of technology

    Technology changes who holds power

    Open data lets ordinary people check what only auditors could before. In 2025, a public map and report form drew more than 20,000 citizen reports in three months.

    Technology and democratic participation

    Access decides who benefits

    A portal helps only those with internet, data skills, and time. Without them, transparency stays on paper.
