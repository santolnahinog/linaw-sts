Corruption survives where money is hard to follow.

Before building a tool against corruption, you need to know how it works. This page covers the causes, the costs, the people involved, and one real case: the 2025 flood control scandal.

What corruption is

The abuse of entrusted power for private gain.
Transparency International's standard definition

In public works, that means money meant for a flood wall, a road or a classroom ends up enriching someone instead.

It rarely looks like a suitcase of cash. More often it hides inside ordinary paperwork: a vague project description, a contract only one firm bid for, or a report that says completed for something never built.

Ghost projectsPaid for and reported done, but never built or impossible to find.
Substandard workBuilt with weaker materials or less work than the contract pays for.

Why it keeps happening

No single bad actor explains it. These five conditions, all visible in the 2025 case, make corruption easier and detection slower.

Choices are made where few can see

Which projects get funded, where, and for how much is decided in steps the public rarely follows. When descriptions are vague, nobody can judge whether a project makes sense.

About 6,021 projects worth over ₱350 billion did not specify the type of work, and some in different places had the same cost. PhilSTAR Life, 11 Aug 2025

A few firms win most of the work

When competition is thin, prices go untested and influence can matter more than quality or cost.

The President said 15 contractors cornered about 20%, or nearly ₱100 billion, of ₱545 billion. Philstar, 16 Aug 2025

Checks come late

Projects are reported complete long before anyone verifies them. The ghost projects were confirmed only after the program had been promoted as a success.

The 2024 SONA cited over 5,500 completed projects. By October 2025, 421 were confirmed as ghost projects. Philstar, Asia News Network

Enforcement is slow and limited

Finding a problem is not the same as resolving it. Investigators need power, independence and time.

By 21 Nov 2025 only one ICI referral had moved forward. Analysts warned the body had limited power. Philstar, SCMP

Citizens can't easily check or report

People closest to a failed project need accessible data and a way to act on what they see.

The public report site drew over 20,000 reports within three months of launching on 11 Aug 2025. Inquirer

The costs reach far beyond the stolen money.

Corruption takes money once, then keeps costing the country through slower growth, stalled projects and lost trust.

Slower growth

The economy grew 4% in the third quarter, a four-year low, as public construction fell. The BSP governor estimated the scandal shaved about 0.3 percentage points off 2025 growth.

PSA via Rappler. BSP via Daily Tribune, Feb 2026
Public building stalls

Public infrastructure spending fell 41.9% year on year in the fourth quarter. The World Bank cut its 2026 growth forecast to 3.7% from 5.3%, linking the slowdown to audits and heavier scrutiny of infrastructure projects.

Daily Tribune. Rappler on the World Bank update
Weaker confidence at home

Officials said households postponed purchases. Household spending grew just 0.5% in the third quarter, and unemployment held at 4.4% in December amid construction job losses.

Rappler
Safety and trust

The projects were meant to protect communities from floods. The President himself called the program's results failures after heavy rains, and the country's corruption score fell to 32.

PhilSTAR Life. Transparency International

Who is involved

Eight groups shape what happens to a public project. Each one needs something different from technology, which is what Linaw's design has to respect.

Citizens and communities

Pay for projects through taxes and live with the results. The first to notice when something was never built.

Needs plain-language data and an easy way to report.

DPWH

Builds and manages public works. In 2025 it reported inspecting about 8,000 flood control projects.

Needs to keep project data open and current.

Contractors

Bid for and build projects. The President said 15 firms won about 20% of flood control value.

Needs clear rules and public bid results.

Lawmakers

Approve the budget and fund projects in their districts. Some have been linked to allegedly anomalous projects, which are under investigation.

Needs published records of budget changes.

Commission on Audit

Audits government spending. It submitted Fraud Audit Reports on flood control projects to the ICI in 2025.

Needs data it can cross-check at scale.

Independent Commission for Infrastructure

Set up to investigate flood control anomalies and refer cases for charges.

Needs evidence in a form it can use.

Office of the Ombudsman

Investigates and prosecutes public officials. It filed cases at the Sandiganbayan in November 2025.

Needs well-documented complaints.

Journalists and civil society

Investigators outside government dig into contracts and budgets, often using public data.

Needs open data they can analyse.

Many of these cases are still in court or under investigation. Anyone named in an allegation is presumed innocent until proven guilty. Linaw does not name individuals.

The rules that already exist

The Philippines does not lack anti-corruption laws. These are the ones most relevant to public works.

1987 Constitution, Article XIAccountability of public officers

Creates the independent Office of the Ombudsman, the principal body for investigating and prosecuting graft.

Republic Act 3019Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, 1960

Defines corrupt practices by public officers and sets penalties for them.

Republic Act 6770Ombudsman Act of 1989

Sets out the Ombudsman's powers and organization, including acting on complaints against public officials.

Republic Act 9184Government Procurement Reform Act, 2003

Makes competitive bidding the default and calls for a single electronic portal as the main source of procurement information. Updated by the New Government Procurement Act, Republic Act 12009, in 2024.

Executive Order 2Freedom of Information, 2016

Lets anyone request information from the executive branch, including through the eFOI portal at foi.gov.ph.

The rules exist. What is missing is an easy way for ordinary people to see them at work. That gap is what Linaw is built for.
