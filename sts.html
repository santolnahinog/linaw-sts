Technology doesn't fix corruption. How people build and use it does.

Science, Technology and Society (STS) asks who a technology is built by, who it serves, and who it leaves out. Here is how those questions apply to this case, and what they mean for the design of Linaw.

Six concepts, one case

Each concept is tied to evidence from the flood control scandal, then to a choice we made in Linaw.

Social construction of technology

Bijker and Pinch

The idea

Technologies are not neutral inventions. Their design reflects the goals, limits and compromises of the groups who build and fund them.

In the flood control case

The same information problem was answered twice, in different shapes. Sumbong sa Pangulo covered flood control only. The DPWH Transparency Portal later expanded the idea to all DPWH projects. What data appears, and in what form, was a choice.

PCO. Philippine Information Agency

How Linaw responds

Linaw shows where its data comes from, says what it leaves out, and labels sample records as samples.

Technological determinism versus social shaping

Does technology act alone?

The idea

Determinism says technology drives change by itself. Social shaping says laws, institutions and politics decide what a technology achieves.

In the flood control case

A public project map and report form went live within two weeks of the President's speech. Yet by 21 November 2025 only one ICI referral had moved forward, and analysts warned the commission lacked power. Technology surfaced problems. People with authority had to act.

Philstar. South China Morning Post

How Linaw responds

Every Linaw tool ends at an official channel, not at a verdict. We claim to inform, not to solve.

Science as evidence and verification

Checking claims against reality

The idea

Science is a way of testing claims against evidence. Technology extends how far and how fast we can test them.

In the flood control case

Teams inspected about 8,000 projects and confirmed 421 as ghost projects. On 3 November 2025, DPWH and the Philippine Space Agency agreed to use satellite imagery and analytics to monitor and validate projects, reducing the need for on-site inspection.

Asia News Network. PhilSA. DZRH

How Linaw responds

Linaw's warning signs come from documented procurement red flags, and every result tells you to verify on the ground.

Public participation in science and technology

Citizens as contributors

The idea

People without formal expertise can contribute real knowledge, especially about their own communities, when a system is designed to receive it.

In the flood control case

More than 20,000 reports arrived in three months. Reports could include photo or video proof and returned a reference number for tracking. The portal lets users tag a project as completed, defective, duplicate, unfinished or ghost.

Inquirer. PhilSTAR Life

How Linaw responds

The Report Guide teaches what makes a report useful: the place, the date, a photo, and exactly what looks wrong.

The digital divide

Access shapes who benefits

The idea

A technology helps only people who can reach and use it. Unequal access can turn a tool for openness into one more advantage for those who already have advantages.

In the flood control case

Only about 28% of Filipino households had fixed broadband in 2023, and wealthier households gained access faster than poorer ones between 2019 and 2022. The people living beside a failed project may be the least connected.

World Bank data, via The Borgen Project and BusinessWorld

How Linaw responds

Linaw is mobile-first, light and written in plain language. A Filipino version and barangay help desks are on our future-improvements list.

Ethics, risk and responsibility

Who could be harmed?

The idea

Tools that score or flag projects and people can cause harm when they are wrong. Responsible design asks who could be hurt, and how mistakes are handled.

In the flood control case

Cases are still before the courts and those accused are presumed innocent. The Palace itself urged citizens not to lie in reports submitted to the public site. Personal information is also protected by the Data Privacy Act of 2012.

Inquirer. Republic Act 10173

How Linaw responds

Linaw says risk, not guilt. It names no people, collects no personal data, and sends users to official channels.

Who gets left out

A website against corruption quietly assumes the reader is online. In the Philippines, that assumption is not safe.

Six design principles that follow

These are the commitments the STS lens turned into design decisions for Linaw.

Show sources and gapsEvery number is cited. Sample data is always labelled as sample.
Say risk, not guiltA score is a prompt to look closer, never an accusation.
End at official channelsEach tool points to the bodies that can investigate and act.
Work on a basic phoneLight pages, readable on a small screen, with no accounts or downloads.
Collect nothing personalNo names, no sign-ups. The Report Guide prepares a message on your own device.
Teach, don't just scoreEach result explains why, so people learn to read project data themselves.

What Linaw cannot do

A fair STS analysis includes the limits of our own technology.

It cannot prove corruptionOnly investigators and courts can. Linaw does not replace the COA, the ICI or the Ombudsman.
Its project records are samplesThis is a prototype. The tracker uses illustrative data, not live government records.
Its risk signs are simplifiedReal procurement review needs documents, context and expertise.
It still needs a device and a signalOffline and Filipino-language versions are future work.
