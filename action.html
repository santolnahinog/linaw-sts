Before you act: four steps

Good evidence and a calm approach make a report far more likely to be taken seriously, and they keep you safe.

  1. 1

    Look safely

    View the project from a public road or open space. Never enter a restricted site, and never confront workers or officials.

  2. 2

    Record the facts

    Note the date, the exact place, any project name or ID you can see, and what looks wrong. Take dated photos or video from where you stand.

  3. 3

    Describe, don't accuse

    Say what you saw, not who you think is responsible. Facts can be checked. Accusations can harm innocent people and weaken a real case.

  4. 4

    Report officially

    Send it to an official channel below. Keep a copy of your report and any reference number you receive.

DPWH Transparency PortalBest for a specific DPWH project

Launched in November 2025. Search any DPWH project, see its implementing office, contractor, cost and status, and report suspected irregularities.

Sumbong sa PanguloBest for flood control projects, while it is open

A report can run to 5,000 characters. Name and contact details are optional, you can attach photo or video, and you receive a reference number. Officials said it would fade out as reports moved to the ICI, so check that it is still accepting reports.

After you report

Reporting is the start of a process, not the end. Here is what to expect and how to protect yourself.

Keep your records

Save your reference number, a copy of your report, your photos, and the date you sent it. If you need to follow up, you will have everything in one place.

Expect it to take time

Investigations are slow. By 21 November 2025, only one referral from the flood control probe had moved forward. Follow up politely, using your reference number.

Philstar, 21 Nov 2025

Protect yourself

On the Sumbong sa Pangulo site, name and contact details were optional, so you could report without giving your name. Never post accusations online. If you fear retaliation, tell the agency and ask what protection is available.

Presidential Communications Office

More ways to help

Reporting is one way. Making public spending easier to see is another.

Look up projects in your own communitySearch your barangay or city on the DPWH Transparency Portal and compare the records with what you see.
Ask for opennessAsk your barangay or local government to post project details where residents can see them.
Teach one personShare the Red Flag Detective with a classmate, a relative or a neighbour who has never read a project record.
Stay curious, stay fairQuestion the data, not people's character. Wait for courts to decide guilt.
